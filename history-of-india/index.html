Scope & method

This page compresses roughly seventy millennia of an enormously large and regionally varied history into twenty-five national chapters and five regional threads. Each map projects the real coastline and state boundaries of the subcontinent and shades the era's major kingdoms in distinct colours where more than one power shared the stage — an approximation of where authority was concentrated, not a precise historical boundary, and pre-modern polities rarely had the hard edges a modern map implies. Population figures before the 1951 Census of India are historical-demography estimates with wide error margins, shown on a log scale precisely because the uncertainty and the range are both enormous. Dates before the Common Era are approximate and, for the earliest periods, drawn from archaeological dating. Names and events are chosen to be representative, not exhaustive — whole dynasties, languages and regional histories had to be left out for space. Each map also shows the subcontinent’s major rivers, and city markers name key centres of the era. Political boundaries follow the official map of India as published by the Government of India, with Jammu & Kashmir and Ladakh shown in full as Indian territory.
